00

One instruction, forty disguises

Read the memos in sequence and the same correction keeps returning — in backbends, in bakasana, on the ball, walking the stairs. It is never really about the pose. It is about where the work lives.

Give the foundation to the legs, and the back has no reason to tense.

When the legs carry the structure, the diaphragm and psoas do their job so quietly you barely feel them — and the chronic grip on your right side loses its reason to exist. Tuck the tail or squeeze the glutes and you do the opposite: you weld the pelvis to the legs, force a hinge in the low back, and stay stuck.

01

How he wants it to work

A · the engine

Diaphragm is the conductor; legs are the orchestra

The diaphragm doesn't do the lifting — it starts a wave. Practice on a half-breath so it never fully relaxes and always holds tone. It gives the command; the legs execute.

"Диафрагма — это дирижёр, а ноги — исполнитель."
B · the pathway

Impulse runs down, not into the spine

Navel in, mula bandha engaged, and the pressure travels diaphragm → psoas → adductor → knee → foot. Lose the path and the force leaks sideways into the low back.

C · the backbend

Carry the pelvis with the ribcage

A real backbend arcs the spine over stable legs and takes the pelvis along. The test: no tension at all down the back. If the pelvis stays behind, you hinge — usually L5–S1.

D · the balance

Adductor up, quadriceps down — 50 / 50

Inner thigh sends up toward the lower ribs; quad releases down below the knee. Don't overload the quad. "Inflate" the front of the hip and let it release deep in the joint.

E · the fine tool

Mula bandha is attention, not tension

Not a clench of the glutes — a precise, quiet link between torso and legs. Pull exactly what's needed and nothing extra. It's what dissolves the outer armor of tension.

"Не tension, а attention."
F · the ground

Engage from the foot up

Not just the thigh — the big-toe-to-heel line, an active shin, the inner knee. That's what switches the adductor on. Unstable foot = you can't carry the pelvis forward.

02

Turn the pelvis from a trapezoid into a square

His most repeated image. Nutation — wings of the pelvis forward and slightly inward — everywhere, in every pose. Counter-nutation (tucking the tail) is the enemy: it turns the torso off from the legs.

Trapezoid · counter-nutation
Tail tucked, glutes gripped. Torso cut off from the legs; the low back takes the hit.
Square · nutation
Wings forward & inward, stabilized. The legs can now drive; the back stays silent.
03

Your right side, read out loud

The pattern

A chronic spasm in the right iliopsoas, just under the ribs on the back-right — higher than the low back. You've traced it to years of football landing in exactly that region.

His hypothesis about the hernia

Instead of shifting load into the right leg, you drove down with the diaphragm and created excess pressure low on the right. "Something there wasn't right — and a hernia happened."

The trap to avoid

Don't relieve the psoas by tensing the glute. That's tension answering tension — now you have two. The grip has simply become so habitual you no longer know how to drop it.

The way out isn't more effort. It's mula bandha giving a floor the old tension can release onto — then attention, gently, taking the tension away.

℞ What to keep doing SUBJECT · right iliopsoas
METHOD · Anatoly Zenchenko / Y23
REFILLS · daily

  1. Load the right leg

    Sit and stand on the right hip joint / sub-gluteal fold, stable knee, weight into the leg. Carry the right wing of the pelvis into the leg — don't leave it behind.

    cue · "переложи крыло таза в правую ногу"

  2. Pull mula bandha — precisely, gently

    Navel in, lower ribs floating up. This is the deep torso-to-leg link, not a glute squeeze. Exactness, not force.

    cue · attention, not tension

  3. Let the diaphragm start the wave

    Work on a half-breath so it keeps tone. It gives the command; send the impulse down through the psoas into the legs — don't press it downward into the belly.

  4. Never tuck the tail

    Keep nutation everywhere — pelvis a square, not a trapezoid; wings forward and slightly inward. Tucking welds pelvis to legs and forces the low-back hinge.

  5. In backbends, carry the pelvis forward

    Arc the spine over stable legs and take the pelvis with it. Adductor up, quadriceps releasing down — 50/50. Don't overload the quad.

  6. Engage from the foot

    Big-toe-to-heel line, active shin, inner knee, adductor. Especially before going onto the hands. Unstable foot = the pelvis can't come forward.

  7. Drill triangles daily

    Trikonasana is the master exercise for making the legs control the pelvis. He rates it the single best teacher of this whole mechanism.

  8. Be patient with the wiring

    Understanding it and doing it are different; the nerves need reps. Progress is circular — "like vacuuming: wipe here, something's left there. Each pass, better."
